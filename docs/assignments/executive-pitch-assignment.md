# Investor pitch deck requirements

Source: assignment text supplied by Haydn on October 30, 2026, with subsequent in-class timing correction.

- 10–15 main slides, excluding backup slides.
- **14-minute total presentation**, superseding the original 10–12-minute guidance. Reserve **3 minutes for the product demo**.
- Use **Focal Family (Prototype B)** for the final presentation.
- Every team member must speak.
- Pitch investors for funding with a persuasive story, not a business-model progress update.
- Explain the problem, why the team chose it and the unique value proposition.
- Identify users, paying customers, willingness-to-pay assumptions and segment size.
- Show the solution and distinguish present capabilities from future work.
- Compare specific alternatives, their strengths and limitations, and the proposed basis for winning.
- Explain revenue models and go-to-market channels.
- Show revenue and expenses over time, including COGS and OPEX assumptions.
- Connect the funding request to uses of funds and milestones.
- End with the amount requested and proposed terms, such as equity offered.
- Make the story understandable to panelists who have not seen earlier coursework.
- Prepare backup slides for investor questions and submit the presentation file to Canvas.

The assignment also describes a **separate** 3–5-page business-plan executive summary, plus optional appendices, following the instructor's specific outline. That outline and the Canvas examples were not supplied here. The current deliverable is the pitch deck.
